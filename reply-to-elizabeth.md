# Reply to Elizabeth

Elizabeth.

I read this carefully. More than once.

Before anything else. Your heart is not what I'm questioning. Not in this exchange. Not ever. I see the *kavod* you've carried. Fuller, the languages, the decades, the *lev* that put you there in the first place. That is not on the table. It will not be on the table. When I push, I am pushing thought, not heart, and I am pushing it the way one pushes a sibling: because the love is settled first.

About the reply that disappeared. I'm sorry for that. Whatever it contained, it took something from you to write it, and that's not nothing.

So let me ask, rather than tell. This is a *machloket l'shem shamayim*, an argument for the sake of heaven. Take it as one.

With all you have carried, Fuller, the languages, the decades, what is it you are actually defending here? The covenant community of Messiah, the one that predates every elder board and Sunday morning format? Or the structure built on top of that, the one we have all learned to call "church"? In your reading, are those the same thing, or different things? If they are different, where does the line fall?

When N.T. Wright recovered Second Temple Judaism for Pauline studies, did he go all the way back, or did he stop where his tradition asked him to stop? Where, if anywhere, do you notice the wall? And if you do notice it, which conclusion did the wall keep off the table?

If the body of Messiah predates the institution, and the institution did not create the body but inherited it, what do you owe to the inheritance, and what do you owe to the housing?

I'm not asking to corner you. I'm asking because I think you already know the answers, or you would not have written what you almost posted.

The table is open. Your seat at it is not in question. If you want to bring the exegesis you held back, even the personal and experiential version, I'll read it. That's not a courtesy offer. I mean it.

Shalom v'shalvah. Your brother in the Way, Sergio.
