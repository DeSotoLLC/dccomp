# A Response to Torah-Keeping / Judaizing Theology

## 1. The Distinction Between the Tablets and the Book of the Law

Deuteronomy 31:26 places the "Book of the Law" written by Moses *beside* the ark of the covenant — not inside it — to function as a witness against Israel:

> "Take this Book of the Law and place it beside the ark of the covenant of the LORD your God, that it may be there for a witness against you." (Deut 31:26)

By contrast, only the tablets of the Testimony — the Ten Commandments inscribed by the finger of God — were placed *inside* the ark:

- **Exodus 40:20** — "He took the testimony and put it into the ark."
- **1 Kings 8:9** — "There was nothing in the ark except the two tablets of stone that Moses put there at Horeb."
- **Hebrews 9:4** — names the ark's contents as the tablets of the covenant.

The spatial distinction is not incidental. It signals a categorical difference between the unchanging moral law written by God Himself and the broader ceremonial code Moses recorded — the latter functioning as a temporary witness to Israel's failure under that system.

## 2. Hebrews on the Ceremonial System

Hebrews argues systematically that the sacrificial system, the Levitical priesthood, the tabernacle, and the feasts tied to them were "a shadow of the good things to come, instead of the true form of these realities" (Heb 10:1).

> "When he said above, 'You have neither desired nor taken pleasure in sacrifices and offerings and burnt offerings and sin offerings' (these are offered according to the law), then he added, 'Behold, I have come to do your will.' He does away with the first in order to establish the second." (Heb 10:8–9)

The author could not be clearer: the ceremonial *first* is done away with so the *second* can be established. Paul says the same thing in Colossians: festivals, new moons, and Sabbaths "are a shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ" (Col 2:16–17).

## 3. The Inconsistency of Selective Torah-Keeping

If someone insists Christians must keep the feasts but concedes that the sacrifices are no longer required "because we have Jesus," they have done two things at once:

1. **Affirmed the principle of fulfillment** — that Christ's work supersedes part of the Mosaic system.
2. **Invented a man-made tradition** — choosing which parts of an integrated system to keep and which to drop, a division Scripture nowhere authorizes.

The feasts were *temple feasts*. Passover required a lamb sacrificed at the central sanctuary (Deut 16:5–6). The Day of Atonement required the high priest entering the Most Holy Place with sacrificial blood. Pentecost, Trumpets, and Tabernacles were inseparable from priesthood, altar, and pilgrimage. James warns, "Whoever keeps the whole law but fails in one point has become accountable for all of it" (James 2:10). One cannot dismantle the system and then claim faithfulness to fragments of it.

## 4. Morality Before Sinai; Covenant At Sinai

Moral norms predate Sinai. Cain knew murder was wrong (Gen 4). Joseph called adultery sin against God before any law was given through Moses (Gen 39:9). The Sabbath principle is grounded in creation (Gen 2:2–3). But the *Mosaic covenant* — its priesthood, feasts, dietary laws, and sacrificial structure — began with Moses and was given to one nation as a tutor "until Christ came" (Gal 3:24).

Paul's argument in Romans 4 is decisive: Abraham was justified by faith *before* circumcision, *before* Sinai, *before* the giving of the law. Therefore the promise rests on faith,

> "in order that the promise may rest on grace and be guaranteed to all his offspring — not only to the adherent of the law but also to the one who shares the faith of Abraham." (Rom 4:16)

The whole point of locating the promise *before* Sinai is so that righteousness is by faith and Gentiles can be grafted in (Rom 11) without becoming Jews.

## 5. The Calendar Problem

A practical problem compounds the theological one. The original biblical calendar was anchored to temple observation, priestly oversight, and the Sanhedrin's intercalation. None of that has been operative since the temple was destroyed in AD 70.

What modern Torah-keeping movements call "the Hebrew calendar" is itself a rabbinic computation finalized in the medieval period — a tradition of men by their own standard. Other movements use an Enoch calendar, a Zadok / Qumran calendar, lunar-Sabbath schemes, aviv-barley sightings, or equinox-anchored reckonings — and they do not agree with one another. Adherents argue endlessly over which "appointed times" are correct because there is no apostolic authority to settle the matter — and there is no such authority because the New Testament never commands Gentile believers to keep them in the first place.

> "Therefore let no one pass judgment on you in questions of food and drink, or with regard to a festival or a new moon or a Sabbath. These are a shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ." (Col 2:16–17)

## 6. The Heart of the Issue: Faith Plus Nothing

The gospel is that salvation rests on the finished work of Christ, received by faith alone. Paul wrote Galatians precisely because Judaizers were adding law-keeping to faith:

> "I do not nullify the grace of God, for if righteousness were through the law, then Christ died for no purpose." (Gal 2:21)

> "You are severed from Christ, you who would be justified by the law; you have fallen away from grace." (Gal 5:4)

The Jerusalem Council settled the question for the Gentiles directly (Acts 15): they were not to be burdened with the yoke of the law. To bind Christians to the feasts, to dietary law, or to ceremonial Sabbath observance as a condition of faithfulness is to add to the gospel — and Paul's response to that is severe:

> "But even if we or an angel from heaven should preach to you a gospel contrary to the one we preached to you, let him be accursed." (Gal 1:8)

That is not pious zeal. It is a different gospel.

## 7. Conclusion

Scripture distinguishes between the moral law placed *inside* the ark and the ceremonial code placed *beside* it. Hebrews declares the ceremonial system fulfilled and set aside in Christ. The feasts cannot be detached from the temple, the priesthood, and the sacrifices that gave them meaning; selective Torah-keeping is a tradition of men, not the teaching of the apostles. The promise to Abraham rested on faith before Sinai precisely so it could extend to all nations through Christ. To require believers today to observe feasts, calendars, and ceremonial obligations — especially when no one can even agree which calendar to use — is to yoke them to a shadow of a system that God Himself has fulfilled in His Son.

> "For freedom Christ has set us free; stand firm therefore, and do not submit again to a yoke of slavery." (Gal 5:1)
