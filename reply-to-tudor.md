# Reply to Tudor

Tudor,

You're still arguing inside a framework you haven't proven and won't name.

**1. Show me the distinction.**
You keep insisting "the Bible makes the distinction between the ceremonial and the moral." Show me the verse. Not Hebrews talking about the Levitical *priesthood* and the *sacrificial system* — which is a real and specific category I happily concede was fulfilled in Yeshua. I mean the categorical split itself: *moral law forever, ceremonial law expired.*

You can't show it, because Scripture never makes it. That tripartite division — moral, ceremonial, judicial — is Aquinas, *Summa Theologica* I–II, Question 99, downstream of Augustine, downstream of a Hellenistic instinct to sort universals from particulars. You inherited a Catholic schoolman's grid, projected it onto a Hebrew text, and then accused me of "legalism" when I declined the grid. That isn't a Bible argument. It's a tradition argument wearing a Bible costume.

The Hebrew Scriptures use one word — *mitzvah* — for all of it. The rabbis count 613, but they don't sort them into "still binding" and "expired." Neither did Yeshua. Neither did Paul. *That sorting is yours.*

**2. Yeshua's own words.**

> "Do not think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets; I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them. For truly I tell you, until heaven and earth disappear, not the smallest letter, not the least stroke of a pen, will by any means disappear from the Law until everything is accomplished. Therefore anyone who sets aside one of the least of these commands and teaches others accordingly will be called least in the kingdom of heaven." (Matt 5:17–19)

Heaven and earth are still here. So is the *yod* and the *tag*. And He explicitly warned against "setting aside one of the least of these commands and teaching others accordingly." That is the verse you have to deal with before you tell me which commandments are now optional.

**3. Acts 15 was about circumcision, not Torah-abolition.**
Read what was actually being argued (v. 1): *"Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved."* The question was whether Gentiles must become full Jewish proselytes — convert — to be saved. The Council answered no. Salvation is by faith. Then James lays out four immediate prohibitions (food sacrificed to idols, blood, things strangled, sexual immorality) — and notice where every one of those four comes from: Leviticus 17–18. That is *Torah*, applied to Gentiles, by James, on day one.

And then James gives the reason in v. 21: *"For the law of Moses has been preached in every city from the earliest times and is read in the synagogues on every Sabbath."* Translation: the four are immediate; the rest the new Gentile believers will learn week by week, from Moses, in the synagogue, on the Sabbath. Acts 15 is the *opposite* of the verse you think it is.

**4. Paul kept Torah. The book of Acts says so plainly.**

- Acts 21:24 — the Jerusalem elders ask Paul to take a Nazirite vow and pay for sacrifices precisely "so that everyone will know there is no truth in these reports… but that you yourself are living in obedience to the law."
- Acts 25:8 — *"I have done nothing wrong against the law of the Jews."*
- Acts 28:17 — *"I have done nothing against our people or the customs of our ancestors."*
- 1 Cor 5:8 — *"Therefore let us keep the Festival."* (Passover.)
- Acts 18:21 — Paul leaving Ephesus: *"I must by all means keep this feast that comes in Jerusalem."*

If Torah were abolished, Paul committed perjury under oath in front of Roman governors. If Torah-keeping for Jewish believers is "Judaizing," Paul Judaized the rest of his life and the Jerusalem church endorsed it.

**5. Hebrews is about the priesthood and the sacrifice. That's it.**
Read the book carefully. Hebrews 7 is about the priesthood transferring from Aaron to Melchizedek. Hebrews 8–10 is about the *sacrificial* system — bulls and goats — being insufficient and replaced by the once-for-all blood of Messiah. That is what's "obsolete" in 8:13. The author never argues against the feasts, the dietary laws, or the Sabbath. Not once. You're reading the abolition of the *sacrificial cult* as if it abolished *all of Torah*. It didn't, and the author is too careful to let you do that.

**6. Jeremiah 31 says the *opposite* of what you think.**

> "I will put **my law** in their minds and write it on their hearts." (Jer 31:33)

Same Torah. Different location. From stone to heart. The new covenant isn't "no more Torah" — it's "Torah internalized, by the Spirit, in a people who actually love it." That's Ezekiel 36:27 too: *"I will put my Spirit in you and move you to follow my decrees and be careful to keep my laws."* If the new covenant abolished Torah, those verses wouldn't make sense — and they're the foundational new-covenant texts.

**7. Colossians 2 doesn't say what you want it to say.**

> "Therefore do not let anyone **judge you** with regard to a festival or a new moon or a Sabbath. These are a **shadow** of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ." (Col 2:16–17)

Read who was judging whom. The Colossian believers *were* keeping the feasts and Sabbaths; the surrounding ascetics and proto-Gnostics were judging them for it. Paul's pastoral counsel is *don't let them judge you for keeping these things*. He doesn't say stop keeping them. And the shadow imagery: when the substance arrives at noon, the sundial still casts a shadow. Shadow and substance coexist. The shadow points; it doesn't vanish.

**8. The "yoke" Peter named was rabbinic, not Mosaic.**
Yeshua Himself rebuked the Pharisees for "tying up heavy burdens, hard to bear, and laying them on people's shoulders" (Matt 23:4). Those burdens were the *takkanot* — the rabbinic fence-laws. Then He said *"Take MY yoke upon you… for my yoke is easy and my burden is light"* (Matt 11:30). And John says *"his commandments are not burdensome"* (1 John 5:3). Put it together: the Mosaic instructions in the hand of the Spirit-filled believer are *not* the unbearable yoke. The unbearable yoke is the Pharisaic accretion. Peter in Acts 15 is talking about the rabbinic yoke being placed on Gentile shoulders as a condition of salvation. That's a different conversation than whether Torah is for the family of God.

**9. Galatians is about justification, not sanctification.**
Paul is fighting one specific heresy: *Gentiles must be circumcised and converted to be saved.* He says no — justification is by faith alone. He never says the Spirit-led believer's life looks like Torah-breaking. He says the *opposite*:

> "Do we then nullify the law through faith? May it never be! On the contrary, **we uphold the law**." (Rom 3:31)

> "For sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace. What then? Shall we sin because we are not under law but under grace? **God forbid.**" (Rom 6:14–15)

"Sin" in the very next breath is defined by — what else — Torah. *"I would not have known what sin was had it not been for the law"* (Rom 7:7).

**10. The monergism / synergism charge backfires.**
You said salvation is monergistic, therefore the works are caused by God. Agreed. So when the Spirit writes Torah on the believer's heart (Jer 31, Ezek 36) and the believer walks it out, that *is* the monergistic God producing the obedience He promised. The Torah-keeping Spirit-filled believer is the most monergistic outcome possible. What you call "synergism" is just the visible fruit of the very God you say sovereignly saves. You're at war with your own soteriology.

**11. The eschatology section is a tangent and it's wrong.**
Romans 11 — Paul's whole argument — is that Israel has not been cast off, that the Gentiles are wild branches grafted into a *Jewish* root, and that "all Israel will be saved." Acts 1:6 — the disciples ask the risen Yeshua about the restoration of the kingdom to Israel and He doesn't correct the premise; He says it's not for them to know the timing. Revelation 7 lists 144,000 from the tribes of Israel. The land promises were "everlasting" (Gen 17:8). Calling premillennial Israel-affirming eschatology a "Jesuit invention" is pop-history; the early church was overwhelmingly chiliast (Justin Martyr, Irenaeus, Papias) for the first two centuries before Augustine spiritualized it. The replacement theology you're defending is the late innovation, not mine.

**12. The actual question.**
Strip the labels. The question isn't "are you saved by Torah?" — no one in this conversation thinks that. The question is: *when the Father redeems a child and sends His Spirit, does that Spirit lead the child away from the Father's instructions, or into them?*

Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Yeshua, and Paul all answer the same way. Into them. Written on the heart. Not as a wage. As a love letter from a Father who wrote them in the first place because He wanted His children to flourish.

I'm not asking you to keep Torah to be saved. I'm asking you to stop telling redeemed people that the Father's words to them are now embarrassing.

— Sergio
