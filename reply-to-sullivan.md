# Reply to D. Michael Sullivan

D. Michael,

Let me guess — you're Reformed?

Because the irony is loud. The tradition that put *Total Depravity* at the top of its acronym, the tradition that quotes Romans 3 and Ephesians 2 like a heartbeat, the tradition built on Calvin's insistence that man is not just sick but *dead* — that tradition now wants to scold a writer for "advocacy of our unworthiness" and accuse him of "smashing the goodness of God"?

You can't both confess total depravity in the catechism and call it a "rant" when someone preaches it on a blog. Pick one.

Here is the actual issue: you have confused the gospel with affirmation. They are not the same thing.

The gospel does not begin "you are fine." It begins *"you were dead in your trespasses and sins"* (Eph 2:1). It says *"all have sinned and fall short"* (Rom 3:23). It says *"there is none righteous, no, not one"* (Rom 3:10). It says *"the heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked"* (Jer 17:9). And then — only then — does it announce that Messiah died for the *ungodly* (Rom 5:6). That's not my paraphrase. That's Paul's word.

Strip the diagnosis and you have not preserved grace. You have made grace unnecessary. There is nothing to be saved from in your version of the gospel because you cannot bear to name what is wrong. That isn't good news. That is religious sentimentality wearing the costume of grace.

You called the piece legalism. Legalism is the doctrine that we add works to Christ's to be saved. The piece says the opposite — it strips us of the lie that we have anything to add. What you are calling legalism is *honesty*. You can't tell the two apart anymore.

Yeshua said the hateful man is a murderer and the lustful man an adulterer (Matt 5:21–28). Calling yourself a thief and a murderer in light of His diagnosis isn't theatrics; it's agreement with the Judge. The publican in Luke 18 went home justified *precisely because* he beat his breast and named himself a sinner — and the man congratulating himself on the goodness of God, the one allergic to being diagnosed, did not. Read that parable again. Yeshua told it about this exact instinct.

> "If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us." (1 John 1:8)

That isn't old-time cranky religion. That is the apostle whose own gospel records that Yeshua wept, sweat blood, and was crushed for our iniquities. He didn't bleed for a people who were fine.

You're not defending grace. You're protecting yourself from it.

— Sergio
