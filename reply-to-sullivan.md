# Reply to D. Michael Sullivan

Dr. Sullivan,

I want to take your comment seriously, so instead of restating my position, let me ask you to defend yours. You made specific charges. They deserve specific answers.

**1. You said the piece is "not truth and grace."**
- What do you mean by "truth and grace"?
- John 1:14 says Yeshua was full of *grace and truth* — both. Which of the two have I excluded?
- Can grace stand without truth, in your view? If so, on what text?

**2. You called the piece "legalism."**
- Define legalism.
- Quote the line where the writer tells anyone they must add their works to Christ's atonement in order to be saved.
- If you can't quote that line, what exactly is "the usual legalism" you're naming?

**3. You said the piece "undermines God's word."**
- Which word, specifically?
- Cite a verse the piece contradicts. One will do.

**4. You called it "advocacy of our unworthiness."**
This is the question I most want answered.
- Is Romans 3:10–12 advocacy of our unworthiness?
- Is Isaiah 6:5 — *"Woe is me, I am undone, a man of unclean lips"*?
- Is the publican's prayer in Luke 18:13 — *"God, be merciful to me, a sinner"*?
- Is Paul's *"of whom I am chief"* in 1 Timothy 1:15?
- Is David's *"against you, you only, have I sinned"* in Psalm 51:4?

Where is the line, in your view, between Scripture's own diagnosis of the human heart and what you call "advocacy"? Mark it for me. Because if those texts are not advocacy of our unworthiness, neither is the piece — and if they are, your charge is with the canon, not with me.

**5. You said the writer echoes "the cranks from old time religion."**
- Which cranks?
- Are you indicting Edwards, Newton, Spurgeon, Whitefield?
- Are you indicting Paul, who called himself the chief of sinners?
- Are you indicting Yeshua Himself, who said the hateful man is a murderer and the lustful man an adulterer (Matt 5:21–28)?

Name the lineage you're rejecting. The piece is in it. So is the New Testament.

**6. You said I "get a kick out of smashing the goodness of God."**
- How is God's goodness "smashed" when the writer agrees with God's own assessment of the human heart?
- *"Every intent of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually."* (Gen 6:5)
- *"The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately sick."* (Jer 17:9)
- Did God smash His own goodness when He said these things?
- And what do you do with Exodus 34:6–7, where in the *same breath* that He proclaims His goodness — *"the LORD, the LORD, a God merciful and gracious"* — He says *"who will by no means clear the guilty"*?

If God's goodness includes both halves of that verse, why does your view of His goodness include only one?

**7. You said there is "no good news, goodwill, or good pleasure of the Father" in the piece.**
- Define good news.
- Then show how Paul's logic works without the diagnosis: *"While we were still sinners, Christ died for us"* (Rom 5:8). Strip the antecedent — sinners — and what does the cross accomplish?
- *"If righteousness were through the law, then Christ died for no purpose"* (Gal 2:21). On what is your cross necessary, in a world that doesn't need to hear it is sinful?

**8. You called the piece "a waste of time."**
- By what metric?
- Yeshua said the publican who beat his breast went home *justified* (Luke 18:14). Was that a waste of time?
- *"If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness"* (1 John 1:9). Is confessing sin — the very thing you're objecting to — a waste of time, according to John?

**Closing.**
Answer those eight, plainly and with Scripture, and we will have an actual conversation. Until then, the charges in your comment are assertions without warrants — and the doctrine of sin you are objecting to is not the writer's invention. It is the apostles', the prophets', and the Lord's. Take it up with them.

Grace and peace,
Sergio
