# Reply to D. Michael Sullivan

Dr. Sullivan,

With respect: did you read past the title?

The charges in your comment describe a piece I did not write. Let me place your words next to mine and you tell me where they meet.

**You charged "advocacy of our unworthiness."**
The piece itself reads:

> "The Hebraic anthropology is not 'you are nothing.' The Hebraic anthropology is 'you are something dignified by your Maker, wounded by sin, and being made whole by the One who calls you by name.'"

> "Sin has wounded that image. Sin has not erased it."

> "Confession restores dignity. Groveling steals it and calls the theft humility."

> "The cross does not exist to keep you small. The cross exists to bring you home."

An entire section of the article — *The grovel as a leash* — exists to *reject* worm-theology. If anything, the legitimate critique from your direction would have been that I'm not severe enough on the human condition. I would have understood that complaint. I cannot understand the one you actually made, because the piece argues the opposite of what you charged.

**You charged "no good news, goodwill, or good pleasure of the Father."**
The piece reads:

> "The Father in heaven loves His handiwork, and His handiwork is you. The Hebrew for that love is אהבה (ahavah)… The Greek is ἀγάπη (agapē)."

> "The veil is torn. Walk in."

> "He died so you could be made whole."

> "That is the gospel. Not a ticket. A garden being reopened. A man being put back together. A King who wore the fringes and remembered every commandment of his Father, dying in the place of the thief, so the thief could come home."

If that is "no good news," what would good news sound like to you?

**You charged "legalism."**
The piece reads:

> "Obedience is not the price of the ticket. There is no ticket. Obedience is part of being whole."

> "You do not obey to earn salvation. You obey because you are being made whole."

Legalism is the doctrine that we add our works to Christ's atonement for salvation. The piece explicitly denies that. Quote the line where it teaches what you accused it of, or withdraw the charge.

**You charged "smashing the goodness of God."**
The piece reads:

> "You are His creation. You are His ma'aseh… You are not an accident. You are not garbage. You are something made by Someone, on purpose, and that Someone has not stopped loving His work because the work cracked."

That is not smashing the goodness of God. That is *enlarging* it past the truncated version much of the tradition has handed us.

**A final question.**
The opening line you found offensive — *"I am a thief. I am a murderer"* — is offered as an honest assessment of any of us measured against Torah. Yeshua said the hateful man is a murderer and the lustful man an adulterer (Matt 5:21–28). Paul called himself *"chief of sinners"* (1 Tim 1:15). The publican beat his breast and named himself a sinner — and went home *justified* (Luke 18:13–14). If those texts stand, so does the opening line.

If you read the article, engage its actual argument. If you didn't, please do — then comment from what is on the page rather than from what the title led you to assume was there.

Grace and peace,
Sergio
