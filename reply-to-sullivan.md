# Reply to D. Michael Sullivan

Dr. Sullivan,

Instead of restating my position, let me ask you to defend yours. A few questions.

**1. "Not truth and grace."** John 1:14 says Yeshua was full of *grace AND truth.* Which half have I excluded, and on what text?

**2. "Legalism."** Quote the line where the writer adds works to Christ's atonement for salvation. If you can't quote it, what are you actually naming?

**3. "Advocacy of our unworthiness."** Is Romans 3:10–12 advocacy of unworthiness? Is Isaiah 6:5 — *"Woe is me, I am undone"*? Is the publican's *"God, be merciful to me, a sinner"* (Luke 18:13)? Is Paul's *"of whom I am chief"* (1 Tim 1:15)? Mark the line between Scripture's own diagnosis and what you call advocacy. If those texts are not advocacy, neither is the piece — and if they are, your charge is with the canon, not with me.

**4. "No good news."** Make Paul's logic work without the diagnosis: *"While we were still sinners, Christ died for us"* (Rom 5:8). Strip the antecedent — sinners — and what does the cross accomplish?

**5. "Smashing the goodness of God."** Exodus 34:6–7: God proclaims *"the LORD, the LORD, a God merciful and gracious"* and in the same breath says *"who will by no means clear the guilty."* If His goodness includes both halves, why does yours include only one?

And the lineage you're rejecting — Edwards, Newton, Spurgeon, Paul, the publican, Yeshua Himself, who said the hateful man is a murderer (Matt 5:21–22) — name your indictment plainly. The piece stands with them. So does the New Testament.

Answer those, with Scripture, and we have a conversation.

Grace and peace,
Sergio
